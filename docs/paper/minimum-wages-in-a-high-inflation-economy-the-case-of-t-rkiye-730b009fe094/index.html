高通胀经济中的最低工资：土耳其案例

Minimum Wages in a High-Inflation Economy: The case of Türkiye

Cem Oyvat, Ceyhun Elgin, Adem Yavuz Elveren

摘要

Drawing on a Post-Keynesian framework, this paper examines the impact of minimum wage increases on inflation, unemployment rate, demand, and the trade balance in Türkiye, a country characterized by persistent high inflation. Using monthly data from 2005 to 2024 and Structural Vector Autoregression (SVAR) models, we analyze the relationships between minimum wage adjustments, consumer prices, unemployment, capacity utilization, and trade balance. Our findings indicate that a 10% increase in the minimum wage raises annual inflation by 1.0-2.0 percentage points. This inflationary effect is primarily driven by the cost channel, as opposed to the demand channel. The analysis finds no significant evidence that minimum wage increases influence demand-side factors such as capacity utilization, industrial growth, or retail sales. The impact on unemployment is minimal —0.10 to 0.15 percentage points. Moreover, a 10% rise in the minimum wage generates an extra trade deficit amounting to 0.11–0.27% of GDP.

中文摘要

本文基于后凯恩斯主义框架，考察最低工资上涨对土耳其这一以持续高通胀为特征国家在通货膨胀、失业率、需求及贸易平衡方面的影响。我们使用2005年至2024年的月度数据和结构向量自回归（SVAR）模型，分析最低工资调整、消费者价格、失业、产能利用率与贸易平衡之间的关系。研究发现，最低工资上涨10%会使年度通货膨胀率上升1.0至2.0个百分点。这一通胀效应主要由成本渠道驱动，而非需求渠道。分析未发现显著证据表明最低工资上涨会影响产能利用率、工业增长或零售销售等需求侧因素。对失业的影响极小——为0.10至0.15个百分点。此外，最低工资上涨10%会额外产生相当于GDP的0.11%至0.27%的贸易逆差。
