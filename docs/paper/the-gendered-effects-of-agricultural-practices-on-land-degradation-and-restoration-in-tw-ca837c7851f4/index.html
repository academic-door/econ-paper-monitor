喀麦隆两个对比农业生态区中农业实践对土地退化和恢复的性别化影响

The gendered effects of agricultural practices on land degradation and restoration in two contrasting agro-ecological zones of Cameroon

Bertin Takoutsing, Leigh Winowiecki, Ann Degrande, Mary Crossland, Simon Djakba Basga 等

摘要

Land degradation remains a major challenge to rural livelihoods and ecosystem sustainability across sub-Saharan Africa, with restoration efforts increasingly recognized as both ecological and governance challenges. While growing attention has been paid to gender-responsive restoration, limited research has examined how gendered land governance systems, tenure relations, and socio-ecological contexts shape perceptions of degradation and restoration practices. This study assessed gendered dimensions of land degradation and restoration across two contrasting agroecological zones in Cameroon, Pitoa in the Sudano-Sahelian zone and Balatchi in the humid Western Highlands. Using a mixed-methods approach combining household surveys (n = 320), participatory biophysical assessments using the Land Degradation Surveillance Framework (LDSF), and geospatial analysis, the study examined perceptions of degradation, restoration practices, training participation, and factors influencing restoration adoption among male and female land users. The findings reveal both shared and differentiated perceptions of land degradation between men and women. While there was moderate overall agreement on degradation drivers (Kendall's coefficient of concordance (wt) = 0.64), low within-gender scores (wt = 0.30 for both men and women) highlight substantial intra-group variation. This suggests that gender alone cannot explain the full diversity of perceptions; other intersecting factors, such as age, tenure, education, and livelihood, likely shape individual viewpoints. Women showed stronger engagement in restoration practices linked to soil fertility improvement, including crop rotation, organic manure application, and agroforestry, while men more frequently emphasized market-oriented and input-intensive strategies. However, restoration decisions were strongly shaped not only by gender roles, but also by unequal access to land, labour, financial resources, training opportunities, and decision-making power. The study further demonstrates that access to land does not necessarily translate into secure control over land or restoration benefits, particularly for women operating under customary tenure systems. The results highlight the importance of integrating gender-responsive and rights-based governance approaches into restoration policy and practice. Effective restoration requires not only ecological rehabilitation, but also strengthened tenure security, inclusive participation, and equitable access to restoration resources and benefits. By linking biophysical assessment with gendered land governance analysis, the study contributes to emerging debates on socially inclusive and sustainable land restoration in sub-Saharan Africa. 中文摘要

土地退化仍然是撒哈拉以南非洲农村生计和生态系统可持续性面临的重大挑战，恢复工作日益被视为既涉及生态问题又涉及治理问题的双重挑战。尽管性别响应的恢复日益受到关注，但关于性别化的土地治理体系、产权关系和社会-生态背景如何塑造人们对退化及恢复实践的认知，相关研究仍然有限。本研究评估了喀麦隆两个截然不同的农业生态区——苏丹-萨赫勒带的Pitoa和湿润西部高地的Balatchi——中土地退化与恢复的性别维度。研究采用混合方法，结合农户调查（n = 320）、基于土地退化监测框架（LDSF）的参与式生物物理评估以及地理空间分析，考察了男性和女性土地使用者对退化的认知、恢复实践、培训参与以及影响恢复采纳的因素。研究结果揭示了男性和女性对土地退化的认知既有共性也存在差异。尽管对退化驱动因素的总体一致性中等（Kendall和谐系数（wt）= 0.64），但性别内部的低一致性得分（男性和女性均为wt = 0.30）凸显了群体内部的显著差异。这表明性别本身无法完全解释认知的多样性；年龄、产权、教育程度和生计等其他交叉因素可能共同塑造个体观点。女性在涉及土壤肥力改善的恢复实践中表现出更强的参与度，包括作物轮作、有机肥施用和农林业，而男性则更倾向于强调市场导向和投入密集型策略。然而，恢复决策不仅受到性别角色的强烈影响，还受到土地、劳动力、资金资源、培训机会和决策权力获取不平等的制约。研究进一步表明，土地获取并不必然转化为对土地或恢复收益的安全控制，尤其是在习惯产权制度下经营的女性群体中尤为明显。研究结果强调了将性别响应和基于权利的治理方法纳入恢复政策和实践的重要性。有效的恢复不仅需要生态修复，还需要加强产权保障、促进包容性参与，并确保恢复资源和收益的公平获取。通过将生物物理评估与性别化土地治理分析相结合，本研究为撒哈拉以南非洲社会包容性和可持续土地恢复的新兴讨论做出了贡献。图形摘要下载：见原文
