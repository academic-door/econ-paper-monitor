检测正在展开的关键节点：基于120年报道的基础、测量与应用

Detecting Critical Junctures as They Unfold: Foundations, Measurement, and Applications from 120 Years of Reporting

Michael Callen, Gregory Levy, Saipremnath Muthukumaran, Jonathan Weigel, Noam Yuchtman

摘要

Explaining why some countries grow rich while others remain poor requires knowing how --- and when --- institutions change. A large body of research argues that institutional change often occurs at critical junctures: moments when multiple institutional futures are possible, including a return to the status quo. Yet most empirical work examines critical junctures only in retrospect, risking the omission of pivotal moments when institutions were in flux but ultimately remained unchanged. We develop a new empirical approach to detect critical junctures as they unfold. Our key insight is to understand critical junctures as moments of uncertainty about institutions, which provides a text classifier with a theoretically motivated object to detect in contemporaneous discourse. In this chapter, we apply this idea to the study of historical critical junctures using newspaper text as a rich source of information regarding contemporary beliefs about institutional uncertainty. We translate this insight into a measurement strategy by training a large language model-based classifier, which we apply to the Times of London archive--11.95 million articles spanning 1800 to 2019--while focusing our main empirical analysis on 120 years of reporting from 1900 to 2019. This produces the first long-run, text-based measure of institutional uncertainty grounded in the concept of critical junctures. Compared to established indices of geopolitical and economic risk, our measure innovates on several fronts: (i) it captures high uncertainty even in periods without formal institutional change, consistent with ex-ante definitions of critical junctures; (ii) it provides earlier signals of major global convulsions, such as World War I; (iii) it evaluates candidate critical junctures using a common ex ante measure; and (iv) it opens new avenues for studying how critical junctures shape institutions and long-run development. Today, our Times-based index suggests that institutional uncertainty in the US and UK is as elevated as it was in the early 1940s--a signal missed by other leading measures of risk.

中文摘要

解释为何一些国家走向富裕而另一些国家却陷入贫困，需要理解制度如何——以及何时——发生变迁。大量研究认为，制度变迁往往发生在关键节点：即多种制度未来可能并存（包括回归现状）的时刻。然而，多数实证研究仅以回溯方式考察关键节点，这可能导致遗漏制度处于变动中却最终未发生改变的关键时刻。我们开发了一种新的实证方法，用于实时检测正在展开的关键节点。核心洞见在于将关键节点理解为制度不确定性时期——这为文本分类器提供了具有理论依据的检测对象，使其能在同期话语中识别此类时刻。本章中，我们运用这一理念研究历史关键节点，以报纸文本作为反映当时制度不确定性认知的丰富信息源。通过训练基于大语言模型的分类器，我们将这一洞见转化为测量策略，并将其应用于《泰晤士报》档案库（涵盖1800年至2019年的1195万篇文章），同时将主要实证分析聚焦于1900年至2019年间的120年报道。由此产生了首个基于关键节点概念、以文本为基础的长期制度不确定性度量指标。相较于既有的地缘政治与经济风险指数，本指标在多个方面实现创新：（i）即便在未发生正式制度变迁的时期，也能捕捉到高度不确定性，这与关键节点的事前定义一致；（ii）能更早预警重大全球动荡（如第一次世界大战）；（iii）采用统一的事前度量标准评估候选关键节点；（iv）为研究关键节点如何塑造制度与长期发展开辟新路径。当前，基于《泰晤士报》的指数显示，美英两国的制度不确定性已升至与20世纪40年代初相当的水平——这一信号被其他主流风险指标所遗漏。
