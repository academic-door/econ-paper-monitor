预防暴力极端主义的发展路径：实证证据的系统综述

Development approaches for preventing violent extremism: A systematic review of the empirical evidence

Leanne M. Kelly, Greg Barton, Anthony Ware

摘要

This systematic review examines English-language publications documenting primary-level community programming for preventing and countering violent extremism (P/CVE) undertaken by development civil society and non-government organisations (CSO/NGOs). The objective is to identify and critically analyse the strengths, problems, gaps, and opportunities in incorporating P/CVE into international and community development work. This focus is necessary because primary-level interventions target broad community resilience, unlike secondary and tertiary interventions, which focus on at-risk or radicalised individuals. Given that development CSOs working on P/CVE programs operate independently from government-led counterterrorism efforts, this review provides insights specific to non-securitised, community-level, development-driven approaches. Source inclusion parameters were set to require empirical studies on primary-level P/CVE programs run by development CSOs. Studies of secondary and tertiary interventions, government-led programs, and initiatives by educational or religious institutions were excluded to maintain the focus on non-state, community-level, development-driven approaches. There was no restriction on location, publication date, or publication type, with both peer-reviewed and grey-literature publications included. As the P/CVE field is relatively new, it was expected that the search would mostly find publications from the 2010s and 2020s, which proved to be the case, with the earliest publication being from 2008. Records were identified using Google Scholar, Google, JSTOR Security Studies, 3ie, ALNAP, OECD iLibrary, and institutional websites, with a final search occurring on 31 January 2024. Bias was assessed by recording authors’ affiliation with the evaluand (although internal evaluations were not excluded on that basis). Extracted data was analysed using grounded theory to inductively determine themes. The systematic review incorporates 129 studies reported in 137 papers: 64% qualitative, 32% mixed-methods, and 4% quantitative. Of these, 53% were industry and evaluation reports, 31% articles, 10% books/chapters, and 6% theses. Limitations include the heterogeneity of included studies and limited evaluation rigour, which constrain reliable conclusions of program effectiveness in many papers. Grounded analysis identified three overarching thematic groupings: interconnections, power and relations, and impact. Common findings across papers highlighted the opportunities for P/CVE programming to enhance the work already being conducted by development CSOs, although papers cautioned that this needs to be carefully designed, implemented, and assessed with consideration of context, objectives, appropriate terminology, local agency, sustainability, marginalised groups, and potential for perverse outcomes. The need for consistent, long-term, untied funding of development CSOs’ P/CVE programs was frequently raised, as was the importance of prioritising local participation in all aspects of programming. Many papers noted the value of effective coordination and collaboration horizontally between communities and CSOs as well as vertically between global and local authorities and academia. Evidence of effectiveness was heterogeneous, with some studies measuring impact and others measuring satisfaction. This likely underlies a particular problem with monitoring and evaluation in this nascent field rather than necessarily representing problematic programming. Key issues with monitoring and evaluation typically include unclear and infeasible theories of change, poor monitoring practices, and limited ability to conduct rigorous evaluation due to insufficient funding and capacity. The most promising practices centred on the evaluation of changes in knowledge, attitudes, social cohesion, and wellbeing. Rather than trying to evaluate the impact of a development program on a reduction in violent extremism, these evaluations demonstrated how P/CVE programs influenced the conditions that facilitate violent extremism and sought to enhance presumed protective factors. While the mechanisms that link these outcomes to P/CVE certainly require further investigation, there is evidence emerging that these preventative programs can strengthen individual and community resilience.

中文摘要

本系统综述梳理了以英文发表的、由发展型民间社会组织与非政府组织（CSO/NGO）实施的、旨在预防和应对暴力极端主义（P/CVE）的初级社区项目文献。研究目标在于识别并批判性分析将P/CVE纳入国际与社区发展工作的优势、问题、空白与机遇。之所以聚焦初级干预，是因为其针对的是广泛的社区韧性，而二级和三级干预则聚焦于高风险或已激进化个体。鉴于从事P/CVE项目的发展型CSO独立于政府主导的反恐行动，本综述提供了针对非安全化、社区层面、发展驱动型方法的独特见解。文献纳入标准要求必须包含发展型CSO实施的初级P/CVE项目的实证研究。为保持对非国家、社区层面、发展驱动型方法的聚焦，排除了二级和三级干预、政府主导项目以及教育或宗教机构倡议的研究。研究未对地点、发表年份或发表类型设限，同行评审文献与灰色文献均纳入分析。由于P/CVE领域相对新兴，预期搜索结果主要来自2010年代和2020年代——事实确如此，最早文献发表于2008年。通过Google Scholar、Google、JSTOR Security Studies、3ie、ALNAP、OECD iLibrary及机构网站检索记录，最终检索日期为2024年1月31日。通过记录作者与被评估对象的隶属关系评估偏倚（但内部评估未因此被排除）。采用扎根理论对提取数据进行归纳性主题分析。本系统综述纳入129项研究（报告于137篇论文中）：64%为定性研究，32%为混合方法研究，4%为定量研究。其中53%为行业与评估报告，31%为期刊文章，10%为书籍/章节，6%为学位论文。局限性包括纳入研究的异质性及评估严谨性不足，这导致许多论文中关于项目有效性的可靠结论受限。扎根分析识别出三大主题群：相互关联性、权力与关系、影响。论文中的共同发现凸显了P/CVE项目增强发展型CSO现有工作的机遇，但论文也警示需结合情境、目标、恰当术语、地方能动性、可持续性、边缘群体及潜在负面后果进行审慎设计、实施与评估。发展型CSO的P/CVE项目需要持续、长期、无附加条件的资金支持这一诉求被频繁提及，同时强调在项目各环节优先保障地方参与的重要性。许多论文指出，社区与CSO之间的横向协调合作，以及全球与地方当局及学术界之间的纵向协作具有重要价值。有效性证据呈现异质性：部分研究测量影响，部分则测量满意度。这很可能反映了这一新兴领域监测评估中的特定问题，而非必然代表项目设计缺陷。监测评估的关键问题通常包括：变革理论不清晰且不可行、监测实践薄弱、因资金与能力不足导致严格评估受限。最具前景的实践聚焦于知识、态度、社会凝聚力与福祉变化的评估。这些评估并非试图衡量发展项目对减少暴力极端主义的影响，而是展示P/CVE项目如何影响助长暴力极端主义的条件，并致力于增强假定的保护性因素。尽管将这些结果与P/CVE关联的机制仍需进一步探究，但已有证据表明，这些预防性项目能够增强个体与社区的韧性。
