不多不少：撒哈拉以南非洲可持续普及电力接入的“金发姑娘”路径

Not Too Much, Not Too Little: A Goldilocks Approach to Sustainable, Universal Electricity Access in Sub-Saharan Africa

摘要

This paper proposes a replicable regulatory and financial framework to achieve universal electricity access as a core component of a just energy transition in Sub-Saharan Africa. It argues that universal access is feasible under realistic constraints when electrification planning, tariff regulation, and capital mobilization are designed as one coherent system rather than separate workstreams. The analysis rests on a computational, multi-period financial model that integrates individual interacting sub-models for every actor in the distribution segment. Versions of the same overall approach are applied to three countries, spanning different starting points and sector structures. Uganda provides the reference implementation in the wake of an expiring private distribution concession; Zambia recalibrates the same logic to a distressed national utility and a thin domestic revenue base; and Madagascar—where the framework is already being applied—shows it moving from design to district-scale delivery in the lowest electricity access setting of the three. The central mechanism is an integrated national planning, regulatory, business, and financing architecture that coordinates grid extension, mini-grids, and stand-alone systems within a single distribution-level financial plan. The framework separates two gaps that are routinely mixed—the viability gap, the recurring shortfall between affordable tariff revenues and the regulated cost of service, and the financing needs, the timing mismatch between front-loaded investment and gradual cost recovery—and shows that they each call for a distinct instrument. The evidence indicates that universal access can be reached without permanent reliance on external grants, and that extending the access horizon rather than compressing investment improves feasibility while still reaching everyone—so that the design becomes a problem of finding an electrification target year, a pattern of overall subsidy, and tariffs that are just right, each neither too much nor too little. The paper reframes universal access as a challenge of cost allocation and institutional design, showing that affordability, inclusion, and financial sustainability are not competing objectives once electrification is designed as one system.

中文摘要

本文提出了一套可复制的监管与金融框架，旨在将普遍电力接入作为撒哈拉以南非洲公正能源转型的核心组成部分。文章主张，在现实约束下，当电气化规划、电价监管与资本动员被设计为一个连贯体系而非各自独立的工作流程时，普遍接入是可行的。分析基于一个计算性的多期金融模型，该模型整合了配电环节中每个参与者的独立交互子模型。同一总体方法的多个版本被应用于三个国家，覆盖了不同的起点和行业结构。乌干达提供了在私营配电特许经营权到期后的参考实施案例；赞比亚将同样的逻辑重新校准应用于陷入困境的国家公用事业和薄弱的国内收入基础；而马达加斯加——该框架已在当地实施——展示了其从设计阶段推进到区域规模交付的过程，且该国的电力接入水平在三国中最低。核心机制是一个集成的国家规划、监管、商业和融资架构，在单一的配电层面财务计划中协调电网延伸、微型电网和独立系统。该框架区分了两种常被混淆的缺口——可行性缺口，即可负担电价收入与监管服务成本之间的经常性短缺，以及融资需求，即前期投资与逐步成本回收之间的时间错配——并表明它们各自需要不同的工具。证据表明，无需永久依赖外部赠款即可实现普遍接入，且延长接入时间跨度而非压缩投资可提高可行性，同时仍能覆盖所有人——因此设计问题转化为寻找一个恰到好处的电气化目标年份、整体补贴模式及电价，每一项既不过多也不过少。本文将普遍接入重新定义为成本分配与制度设计的挑战，表明一旦电气化被设计为一个整体系统，可负担性、包容性和财务可持续性便不再是相互竞争的目标。
