发展型国家下的嵌入式不公：台湾对公正转型的反身治理

Embedded injustice under the developmental state: Taiwan's reflexive governance on just transition

Kuei-Tien Chou, Jui-Ning Chang

与中国相关环境与气候发展经济学劳动

摘要

Taiwan has incorporated just transition into net zero governance, but implementation proceeds through institutions shaped by developmental state industrialization. This study examines how labor and environmental movements contributed to legal development and how unresolved legacies shape a just energy transition. It combines thematic and doctrinal legal analysis with a purposive comparison of ten labor and ten environmental cases, including one negative institutional comparator, and six net zero transition cases: the carbon fee, high carbon industrial dependence, worker and regional support, fishery–solar co-location, offshore wind, and geothermal development. Historical cases indicate that protections often strengthened only after collective action exposed occupational, livelihood, pollution, and territorial harms. Reforms expanded rights and administrative review, yet responsibilities remained fragmented, remedies delayed, and red–green relations weakly institutionalized. Similar tensions are visible in net zero policy: carbon fee design affords regulated firms adjustment flexibility without equivalent worker or regional safeguards, while renewable energy siting addresses livelihood and territorial claims mainly through permitting, consultation, and compensation. Because the carbon fee is recent, estimates identify structural exposure rather than observed employment or regional effects. The analysis develops brown economy dependence, the sacrifice system, and hidden and delayed governance as mechanisms through which accumulated harms become normalized in legal categories, administrative routines, and development priorities. Embedded injustice reveals how accumulated harms condition net zero governance, sensitizes just transition policy to these legacies, and supports cautious comparison across sectors and developmental state contexts. Reflexive governance in Taiwan therefore requires earlier coordination and revisable decisions.

中文摘要

台湾已将公正转型纳入净零治理，但其实施仍通过由发展型国家工业化所塑造的制度推进。本研究考察劳工与环境运动如何推动法律发展，以及未解决的遗留问题如何塑造公正能源转型。研究结合主题分析与法释义学分析，并对十个劳工案例与十个环境案例进行目的性比较，其中包括一个负向制度对照案例，以及六个净零转型案例：碳费、高碳产业依赖、劳工与区域支持、渔电共生、离岸风电与地热开发。历史案例表明，保护往往只有在集体行动揭示职业、生计、污染与领土损害之后才得到强化。改革扩展了权利与行政审查，但责任仍然碎片化，救济延迟，红绿关系制度化薄弱。类似张力也出现在净零政策中：碳费设计为受管制企业提供调整弹性，却未提供对等的劳工或区域保障；而可再生能源选址主要透过许可、咨询与补偿来处理生计与领土主张。由于碳费实施时间尚短，相关估计识别的是结构性暴露，而非已观察到的就业或区域效应。分析提出褐色经济依赖、牺牲体系，以及隐蔽与延迟治理，作为累积损害在法律范畴、行政常规与发展优先序中被正常化的机制。嵌入性不正义揭示了累积损害如何制约净零治理，使公正转型政策对这些遗留问题更为敏感，并支持跨部门与发展型国家脉络下的审慎比较。因此，台湾的反思性治理需要更早协调与可修正决策。
