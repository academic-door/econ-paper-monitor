私人市场能否填补空白？GradPLUS取消后的研究生贷款

Can Private Markets Fill the Gap? Graduate Student Lending After the Elimination of GradPLUS

Sarah Turner

摘要

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21) eliminates the federal GradPLUS loan program for new borrowers effective July 1, 2026, replacing nearly two decades of uncapped federal graduate lending with annual limits of $20,500 ($50,000 for eleven professional fields) and new aggregate caps. The resulting financing gap — approximately $8 billion annually across roughly 370,000 affected borrowers — raises the question of whether and how private capital will substitute for displaced federal lending. This paper analyzes the economics of that transition. Before GradPLUS, private lenders filled the gap between federal Stafford limits and graduate program costs, with nonfederal graduate originations reaching $5.3 billion (constant 2024 dollars) by 2005–06. After GradPLUS extended federal borrowing to the full cost of attendance in 2006, private lending collapsed — falling more than 70 percent within a few years and never recovering. The federal program provided not only credit but, through income-driven repayment and Public Service Loan Forgiveness, insurance against earnings risk that no private product could replicate. The same features that crowded out private lending generated compounding distortions: borrowing untethered to repayment capacity, upward pressure on tuition, and a reversal from projected federal surpluses to losses exceeding thirty cents per GradPLUS dollar lent. Linking institution-by-field federal borrowing data to institution-by-field earnings outcomes, I examine the relationship between historical borrowing above the new OBBBA caps and post-completion earnings across programs. I find substantial variation in the extent to which different degree programs would likely support above-cap borrowing from private lenders or other sources. In fields such as law and MBA, earnings rise steeply with borrowing exposure, providing a basis for program-level risk pricing; in fields such as social work, counseling psychology, and physical therapy, the earnings-to-borrowing gradient is essentially flat. I also examine features of the private capital market and institutional responses shaping the transition — including capital constraints on scaling private lending, legal barriers to using program-level outcomes data in underwriting, and the emerging role of institutions as intermediaries between lenders and students.

中文摘要

《一项重大美丽法案》（OBBBA，公法119-21）自2026年7月1日起取消联邦GradPLUS贷款计划对新借款人的资格，以每年20,500美元（十一个专业领域为50,000美元）的年度限额和新的总限额取代了近二十年无上限的联邦研究生贷款。由此产生的融资缺口——每年约80亿美元，涉及约37万名受影响借款人——引发了私人资本是否以及如何替代被取代的联邦贷款的问题。本文分析了这一转变的经济学逻辑。在GradPLUS计划之前，私人贷款机构填补了联邦斯塔福德贷款限额与研究生项目成本之间的缺口，非联邦研究生贷款发放额在2005-06年度达到53亿美元（按2024年不变美元计算）。2006年GradPLUS将联邦借款扩展至全部就读成本后，私人贷款急剧萎缩——几年内下降超过70%，且从未恢复。联邦计划不仅提供信贷，还通过收入驱动还款和公共服务贷款豁免提供了收入风险保险，这是任何私人产品都无法复制的。正是这些挤出私人贷款的特征产生了叠加的扭曲效应：借款与还款能力脱钩、对学费形成上行压力，以及联邦盈余预期逆转至每发放一美元GradPLUS贷款损失超过三十美分的局面。通过将按院校-专业划分的联邦借款数据与按院校-专业划分的收入结果相匹配，我考察了超出新OBBBA上限的历史借款与各项目毕业后期收入之间的关系。我发现不同学位项目在多大程度上可能支持从私人贷款机构或其他来源进行超上限借款方面存在显著差异。在法律和MBA等领域，收入随借款敞口急剧上升，为项目层面风险定价提供了基础；而在社会工作、咨询心理学和物理治疗等领域，收入与借款之间的梯度基本持平。我还考察了塑造这一转型的私人资本市场特征和机构应对措施——包括私人贷款规模扩张的资本约束、在承保中使用项目层面结果数据的法律障碍，以及机构作为贷款人与学生之间中介的新兴角色。
