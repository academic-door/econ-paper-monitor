Training Frontline Health Workers in Low- and Middle-Income Countries to Reduce Contraceptive Stock-Outs: Evidence from Indonesia

摘要

Frontline health workers are an indispensable asset in the healthcare ecosystem of low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). They not only provide clinical care to patients but also frequently shoulder nonclinical responsibilities such as inventory management. However, many lack the necessary skills to effectively manage inventories, leading to the pervasive problem of health commodity stock-outs. To address this problem, several LMICs have launched training programs aimed at enhancing the inventory management skills and capabilities of frontline health workers. Leveraging fine-grained data over a five-year period (2015–2019) from approximately 17,000 health facilities across Indonesia—where the public health supply chain operates as a hybrid push–pull model—we evaluate the impact of a large-scale inventory management training program on contraceptive stock-outs. Applying difference-in-differences estimations, we find that the training program is associated with a significant reduction in stock-outs (i.e., 6.52 percentage points, or approximately 30%). Our numerical calculations suggest that the training program prevents, on average, more than 800 unintended pregnancies and saves 4.25 maternal and newborn lives per 100,000 women of reproductive age within the catchment area of a treated health facility. A counterfactual benefit–cost analysis shows that on-site delivery of the training program (i.e., on the job within a health facility) yields operational and public health benefits that significantly exceed those achieved through off-site delivery (i.e., outside the health facility in a classroom). Our research highlights the practicality and scalability of human-capital-focused interventions in improving health commodity availability and public health outcomes in LMICs. This paper was accepted by Stefan Scholtes, healthcare management. Supplemental Material: The online appendix and data files are available at https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03696 .

中文摘要

一线卫生工作者是中低收入国家（LMICs）医疗体系中不可或缺的资产。他们不仅为患者提供临床护理，还经常承担非临床职责，如库存管理。然而，许多人缺乏有效管理库存所需的技能，导致卫生产品缺货问题普遍存在。为解决这一问题，多个中低收入国家已启动旨在提升一线卫生工作者库存管理技能与能力的培训项目。利用印度尼西亚约17,000家卫生机构在五年期间（2015-2019年）的细粒度数据——该国的公共卫生供应链采用混合推拉模式——我们评估了一项大规模库存管理培训项目对避孕药具缺货的影响。通过双重差分估计，我们发现该培训项目与缺货率显著下降相关（即下降6.52个百分点，约30%）。我们的数值计算表明，该培训项目平均可预防超过800例意外怀孕，并在受训卫生机构服务区域内，每10万名育龄妇女中挽救4.25名母婴生命。反事实成本效益分析显示，现场实施培训项目（即在卫生机构内进行在职培训）所产生的运营和公共卫生效益显著超过非现场实施（即在卫生机构外的教室环境中进行培训）。我们的研究强调了以人力资本为重点的干预措施在改善中低收入国家卫生产品可及性和公共卫生结果方面的实用性与可扩展性。本文已被Stefan Scholtes在医疗管理领域接受。补充材料：在线附录和数据文件可在https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03696获取。
